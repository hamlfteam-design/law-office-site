Can a defendant in a drug case be released in Egypt? When is release on bail or an alternative measure granted?

By Counselor Dr. Hassan Abdelmoneim Hassan

Can a defendant in a drug case be released in Egypt? When is release on bail or an alternative measure granted?

Yes. In principle, a person held in a drug case in Egypt can be released. Pretrial detention is an exceptional measure, not a punishment, and at every renewal hearing the prosecution or the court decides whether to keep the person in custody or release them on bail, under an alternative measure, or without conditions. In practice the answer depends heavily on the facts: whether the charge is personal use or trafficking, the type and quantity of the substance, the person's record and fixed address, and whether the arrest and search were lawful.

Who decides on release in drug cases?

At first the case is with the Public Prosecution, which questions the suspect and may order detention or release. Once the prosecution's own detention period runs out, the suspect is brought before the judge responsible for renewing detention (the council chamber), and later before the trial court once the case is referred. At each stage the defence may apply for release and present its reasons and documents.

Because most offences under Drug Law No. 182 of 1960 are felonies, cases usually go to the Felony Court, and detention follows the limits that apply to felonies.

What affects the decision?

There is no fixed table that says when a defendant walks out. In practice, prosecutors and judges usually look at:

  • The charge: possession for personal use is treated very differently from trafficking or importation.
  • The quantity and type of substance, how it was seized, and the forensic lab report.
  • Personal circumstances: a fixed address, a job, family, age, health and a clean record.
  • The needs of the investigation: is there a risk of flight or of interfering with witnesses or evidence?
  • Procedural soundness: was the arrest and search carried out under a valid warrant or a genuine in-the-act situation? This can change the whole course of the case.

What do bail and alternative measures mean?

Release may be on a cash guarantee (bail), on a guarantee of residence, or subject to an alternative measure. Article 114 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, Law No. 174 of 2025 (in force since 1 October 2026), sets out the alternatives to pretrial detention, including: an obligation not to leave one's home, reporting to the police station at set times, a ban on frequenting certain places, a ban on possessing weapons, and electronic tracking.

Under Article 115, breaching an imposed measure can lead to pretrial detention, so release conditions must be followed strictly.

If detention keeps being renewed, how long can it last?

Pretrial detention has a ceiling that cannot be exceeded, and it differs by type of offence. For the full picture, see our article What is the difference between pretrial detention and a prison sentence, and how long can detention last?

Article 124 of Law No. 174 of 2025 provides that pretrial detention during the preliminary investigation may not exceed one third of the maximum penalty prescribed for the offence, and in any event may not exceed:

  • four months for misdemeanours;
  • twelve months for felonies;
  • eighteen months where the prescribed penalty is life imprisonment or death.

For felonies, detention beyond five months requires an order from the competent court. The verbatim wording should be checked against the Official Gazette [needs verification].

What should I do if a relative is arrested in a drug case?

  • They have the right to ask for their lawyer to be present before being questioned.
  • Find out the police report number, the police station, the competent prosecution office and the hearing date.
  • Gather proof of residence, employment and health; these support a release application.
  • Do not contact or pressure witnesses; it harms the defendant and can justify continued detention.

Specialist legal opinion

Release in drug cases is rarely decided by the charge alone. It turns on a close reading of the arrest file: the prosecution warrant and its date and time, the seriousness of the police inquiries, whether there was a genuine in-the-act situation, the weight of the seized items against the lab report, and the characterisation of intent (use or trafficking). Any weak link strengthens a release application at renewal hearings and may support substantive defences at trial, depending on the facts and where the legal conditions are met. Relevant Court of Cassation rulings (from the firm's Cassation library): Appeal No. 8676 of Judicial Year 67, session of 12 February 2007 (Technical Office 58, p. 140), on the time the warrant was issued and its consistency with the station log; and Appeal No. 6489 of Judicial Year 81, session of 25 February 2017 (Technical Office 68, p. 181), holding that the seriousness of police inquiries is a matter of fact for the trial court.

Frequently asked questions

Can a defendant in a drug case be released in Egypt?

Yes, in principle. Pretrial detention is exceptional, and the decision depends on the charge, the quantity, the defendant's circumstances and whether procedures were lawful.

Does a personal-use defendant have a better chance than a trafficking defendant?

Usually yes, because the penalty for possession for personal use is lighter than for trafficking, but the decision remains discretionary and fact-specific.

Who decides on release?

The Public Prosecution at first, then the judge who renews detention, then the trial court once the case is referred.

Is bail always required?

No. Release may be on bail, on a guarantee of residence, or with an alternative measure, as the competent authority decides.

Does release mean acquittal?

No. Release only means the defendant follows the case from outside custody; the case continues until judgment.
