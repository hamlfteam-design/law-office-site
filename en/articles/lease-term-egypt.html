Does a Lease Have a Term? When Does It End? And What If No Term Is Stated?

By Ahmed Mohamed El-Naqqadi — Attorney

Does a Lease Have a Term? When Does It End? And What If No Term Is Stated?

Many people ask us: “How long does a lease last?”, “My lease has expired but the tenant will not leave”, “Is a lease without a stated term valid?” and “My landlord wants me out — is that his right?” The answer: the term is an essential element of a lease. Where the parties fix a term, the lease ends upon its expiry without any need for notice. Where no term is fixed, or the term cannot be proved, the lease is deemed concluded for the period set for payment of rent, and it ends only upon a notice to vacate served within the time limits prescribed by law.

What is the term of a lease?

It is the period during which the lessor undertakes to enable the lessee to use the leased property in return for a known rent (Civil Code, Art. 558). Under the Civil Code, a lease should have a determinate term.

The stated term has expired — does the lease end automatically?

Yes. A lease ends upon expiry of its determinate term without any notice to vacate (Civil Code, Art. 598), unless the parties stipulated that notice be given before expiry, in which case that stipulation must be applied.

The term has expired, the tenant remains in the apartment, and the landlord is silent. What is the rule?

If the tenant remains in possession with the lessor’s knowledge and without objection, the lease is deemed renewed on its original terms but for an indeterminate period (Civil Code, Art. 599). Article 563 then applies, and the lease ends only upon a notice to vacate.

The lease says “for life”, “month-to-month” or “as needed”. Is that a term?

No. Such wording is not a determinate term. The Court of Cassation has held that a lease governed by the Civil Code must have a fixed term, and that a lease with no fixed end date, or whose termination depends on the tenant’s will alone, is treated as a lease of indeterminate duration: it is concluded for the period set for payment of rent and ends upon a notice to vacate.

What are the notice periods for a lease of indeterminate duration?

Under Article 563 of the Civil Code:

Type of propertyPeriod set for payment of rentNotice must be served
Agricultural and fallow landSix months or moreThree months before the end of the period
Agricultural and fallow landLess than six monthsBefore the last half of the period
Buildings, dwellings, furnished rooms and other thingsTwo months or moreOne month before the end of the period
Buildings, dwellings, furnished rooms and other thingsLess than two months (e.g. monthly rent)Before the last half of the period

If notice is not served in time, the lease is renewed for the same period, then for a similar one, and so on until notice is served, as settled in the case law of the Court of Cassation.

Is there a maximum term or any special condition?

There is no general maximum, but: (1) a person who holds only a power of administration may not lease for more than three years, and a longer term is reduced to three years (Civil Code, Art. 559); (2) a lease exceeding nine years must be registered so that it may be invoked against third parties.

What is the term of old-rent leases after Law No. 164 of 2025?

Law No. 164 of 2025, in force since 5 August 2025, fixed a transitional period for leases governed by Laws No. 49 of 1977 and No. 136 of 1981: seven years for residential premises (i.e. until approximately August 2032) and five years for non-residential premises leased to natural persons (i.e. until approximately August 2030), unless the parties agree to an earlier termination. When the period ends, the tenant must vacate the premises.

A question you should know the answer to

Question: What is the notice period to vacate if the rent is monthly and the lease has no fixed term?

Answer: If the period set for payment of rent is less than two months, such as monthly rent, notice must be served before the last half of that period (Civil Code, Art. 563).
