When is a defendant's file referred to the Grand Mufti in Egypt, and is a death sentence invalid if his opinion was not sought?

By Counselor Dr. Hassan Abdelmoneim Hassan

When is a defendant's file referred to the Grand Mufti in Egypt, and is a death sentence invalid if his opinion was not sought?

A defendant's file is referred to the Grand Mufti of Egypt when a Felony Court is inclined to impose the death penalty: the court may only do so unanimously and after obtaining the Mufti's opinion. The opinion is advisory and does not bind the court, but failing to seek it at all is a breach of a mandatory procedure that exposes a death sentence to being overturned by the Court of Cassation. Referral is not a death sentence; it is a step before judgment is pronounced at a later hearing.

Why is this in the news?

It was reported on 15 November 2026 that the Sohag Felony Court referred the files of fifteen defendants, in a case arising from a clash between two families, to the Grand Mufti and set 22 December for pronouncing judgment. No judgment has been issued, the case is still pending, and the defendants are presumed innocent until a final judgment. We therefore do not comment on its facts or evidence and only explain the legal rule.

When does the court refer the file to the Mufti?

Article 381 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (the numbering cited in press coverage) provides that a Felony Court may only impose a death sentence by unanimous decision of its members and must first obtain the opinion of the Grand Mufti. The article's number and wording under the new Law No. 174 of 2025, and the time allowed for the Mufti's opinion, must be checked against the official text [needs verification].

In practice, referral signals that the court is provisionally inclined towards the death penalty, but it has not ruled and may impose a different penalty at the judgment hearing.

Is the court bound by the Mufti's opinion?

No. The opinion is advisory: the court must seek it but need not follow it, and the decision remains the court's alone. The obligation concerns the procedure, not the outcome.

Is a death sentence invalid if the Mufti's opinion was not sought?

The rule is that failing to seek the Mufti's opinion, or imposing death without unanimity, taints the judgment and exposes it to cassation for breach of a mandatory procedure [needs verification].

Youm7 reported on 4 November 2026 that the Court of Cassation's General Assembly for Criminal Matters held that the Mufti's opinion must be sought again before the Appellate Felony Court if it is inclined to impose death, departing from an earlier position. We were unable to locate this ruling in the firm's Cassation library, so we mention it only as a press report, not as a verified principle [needs verification].

Does a death sentence go to the Court of Cassation even without an appeal?

Yes, under the rule in force: the Public Prosecution must submit any case ending in a death sentence to the Court of Cassation with a memorandum of its opinion, even if the convicted person does not appeal, and the Court reviews the case of its own motion. The governing article and the deadline for submission must be checked against the current text [needs verification].

Specialist legal opinion

Egyptian law surrounds the death penalty with three linked safeguards: unanimity of the bench, the Mufti's opinion before pronouncement, and mandatory review by the Court of Cassation. A breach of any of them is a substantial ground of challenge. With the creation of Appellate Felony Courts, whether the Mufti must be consulted again at the appeal stage has become a practical question, and the Court of Cassation's latest settled position must be confirmed before any principle is relied on [needs verification].
