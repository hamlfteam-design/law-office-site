What Is the Difference Between Forgery and Counterfeiting Under Egyptian Law?

By Counselor Dr. Hassan Abdelmoneim Hassan — Attorney before the Court of Cassation

What Is the Difference Between Forgery and Counterfeiting Under Egyptian Law?

People often ask us whether "forgery" and "counterfeiting" are the same offence, what the penalty is for forging an official paper, and whether they can be prosecuted for a fake banknote that ended up in their wallet. The short answer: Egyptian law treats them as two separate crimes. Forgery (tazweer) is altering the truth in a document. Counterfeiting (tazyeef) targets money itself: paper or coin currency. Currency offences sit in Articles 202 et seq. of the Penal Code; document forgery sits in Articles 211 et seq., and the penalties differ.

Forgery: altering the truth in a document

The Penal Code recognises two forms:

  • Material forgery leaves a physical trace on the paper. Article 211 lists the methods, including forged signatures or seals, altering documents, seals or signatures, adding words, and inserting forged names or photographs of other people.
  • Intellectual (non-material) forgery changes what a document says at the time it is drawn up, for example recording a false fact as true, without any physical alteration. Article 213 punishes this when committed by a public official drafting an official document.

A fingerprint counts as a signature for the purposes of the forgery chapter (Article 225).

Counterfeiting: copying or falsifying money

Article 202 punishes anyone who counterfeits, falsifies or forges, by any means, paper or coin currency that is legal tender in Egypt or abroad. Article 203 extends this to bringing counterfeit currency into or out of Egypt, putting it into circulation, or holding it with intent to circulate or deal in it. Article 202 bis covers authorised gold and silver commemorative coins.

Penalties for counterfeiting currency

  • Counterfeiting (Art. 202) and importing, exporting, circulating or holding for circulation (Art. 203): aggravated imprisonment.
  • Aggravation (Art. 203 bis): where the offence causes a fall in the value of the Egyptian currency or government bonds, or undermines confidence in the markets, the court may impose life imprisonment.
  • Good-faith recipient who later passes the note on (Art. 204): imprisonment of up to three months or a fine of up to EGP 200, if they dealt in it after learning it was fake.
  • Related offences (Art. 204 bis and following): including making or selling items resembling currency that could mislead the public, and making or holding counterfeiting tools without justification, punishable by imprisonment.

Some forms of forgery have their own provisions and fall outside Articles 211–215, such as travel tickets, medical certificates and inheritance or death declarations (Articles 216–224 and 226).

What if I used a forged document without knowing?

The "use" offences in Articles 214 and 215 require knowledge that the document was forged. If you genuinely did not know, that element is missing, but whether you knew is a question of fact that the prosecution and the court assess from the circumstances. Keep whatever shows where the document came from, and do not submit papers you have doubts about.

Is there any exemption from punishment?

  • Currency (Art. 205): an offender who reports the crime to the authorities before the counterfeit currency is used and before any investigation begins is exempt. If the report comes after the investigation has started, the court may still exempt them if it enabled the arrest of other offenders or of offenders in a similar crime.
  • State seals and marks (Art. 210): a similar exemption applies to whoever reports before the crime is completed and before investigation, and identifies the other offenders or helps arrest them.
  • Presidential pardon and parole are discretionary, governed by other rules, and should not be relied on when assessing a case.

Practical precautions

  • Do not submit a document to any authority if you are unsure where it came from.
  • Never sign someone else's name, even with their verbal permission.
  • If you suspect a banknote is fake, do not pass it on: dealing in it after learning it is fake is an offence (Art. 204). Ask a lawyer how best to hand it over.
  • If you are summoned in a forgery or counterfeiting matter, ask for your lawyer before giving a statement or signing anything.

Specialist legal view

The dividing line is the object of the act. Altering the truth in a document engages the forgery provisions, and the type of document (official, private, or belonging to a company or public-benefit body) together with the offender's status (public official or not) decides which article and penalty apply. Acts directed at currency in circulation fall under Article 202 et seq. In both, the defence turns on the elements of the offence, above all knowledge in use and circulation offences, and on scrutiny of the forensic document-examination report. Any Court of Cassation principle relied on in such cases should be checked against the Court's most recent settled position.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between forgery and counterfeiting in short?

Forgery is altering the truth in a document (official or private) and is governed by Penal Code Articles 211–215; counterfeiting is copying or falsifying paper or coin currency in circulation and is governed by Articles 202–205.

What is the penalty for counterfeiting currency in Egypt?

Aggravated imprisonment for counterfeiting, circulating, or holding counterfeit currency for circulation (Articles 202 and 203); life imprisonment is possible if the offence causes a fall in the value of the Egyptian currency or government bonds or undermines market confidence (Article 203 bis).

Can I be punished for using a forged document I did not know was forged?

The use offences in Articles 214 and 215 require knowledge of the forgery; the prosecution and court assess knowledge from the facts, so keep proof of where the document came from and speak to a lawyer before giving a statement.

I found a fake banknote in my money. What should I do?

Do not spend or pass it on: under Article 204 a person who received counterfeit money in good faith and then deals in it after learning it is fake faces up to three months' imprisonment or a fine of up to EGP 200.
