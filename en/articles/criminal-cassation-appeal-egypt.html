The Criminal Cassation Appeal in Egypt: Deadline, Grounds, Procedure, and Prospects

By Counselor Dr. Hassan Abdel Moneim Hassan, Attorney before the Court of Cassation

The Criminal Cassation Appeal in Egypt: Deadline, Grounds, Procedure, and Prospects

A question we hear often: “I, or a relative, was convicted of a felony, or on a misdemeanor appeal, and we want to appeal to the Court of Cassation. What do we do, and how many days are left?” The short answer: a cassation appeal is an extraordinary appeal with a strict deadline and grounds fixed by law; it is not a third chance to present the case as it was. Your prospects therefore turn on two things: is there a legal or procedural defect in the judgment that can serve as a ground for cassation, and will the appeal be filed on time and in the correct form? This article explains both step by step, and refers you to our earlier article on the difference between opposition, appeal, cassation, and the other ways of challenging a judgment.

First: Which judgments can be appealed to the Court of Cassation?

The Public Prosecution and the convicted person may appeal to the Court of Cassation against final judgments issued by the last instance in felonies and misdemeanors, that is, judgments no longer open to opposition or ordinary appeal: for example, judgments of the Criminal Courts (felonies) and misdemeanor judgments rendered on appeal by the Courts of Appeal. A first-instance misdemeanor judgment that has not yet been appealed goes to the ordinary appeal first, and a judgment in absentia is first challenged by opposition under its own conditions.

Second: Grounds for a cassation appeal

Article 30 of the Law on the Cases and Procedures of Appeal before the Court of Cassation limits the grounds for appeal against criminal judgments to three, and no ground outside them is accepted:

GroundMeaningCommon examples
Violation of the law, or error in applying or interpreting itThe court applies a provision where it does not belong, misreads it, or overlooks a provision that had to be appliedGiving the facts the wrong legal characterization, imposing a penalty outside its limits, overlooking a ground of justification or a bar to punishment
Nullity in the judgmentThe judgment itself is defective in its structure or reasoningDeficient reasoning, contradiction, faulty inference, failure to answer a material defense, failure to state the facts and the evidence
Procedural nullity that affected the judgmentA void procedure occurred during investigation or trial and that nullity affected the outcomeVoid arrest or search, infringement of the right of defense, failure to hear material witnesses, irregular composition of the court or summoning of the accused
The most damaging mistake is to build the appeal on “the evidence is insufficient” or “the witnesses are lying” as such; that is an assessment of the facts, which belongs to the trial court. The objections must be turned into legal defects in the judgment or the procedures, for example that the judgment did not answer a material defense raised by the defense, or based the conviction on evidence derived from a void procedure.

Third: Deadline and procedure

  1. The deadline is sixty days from the date a judgment given in the defendant’s presence is rendered (Art. 34), counted from the day after the judgment; it is a forfeiture period and no appeal is accepted after it.
  2. Declaring the appeal: the appeal is declared at the clerk’s office of the court that issued the judgment, and the record bears a number and date proving it was filed on time.
  3. Grounds memorandum: it must be filed within the same period, signed by a lawyer admitted before the Court of Cassation, and must specify each ground precisely, showing where the defect lies in the judgment and how it affected the result. General, sweeping grounds do not help.
  4. The appeal is then heard by a criminal circuit of the Court of Cassation; the Public Prosecution (the Cassation Prosecution) gives its opinion, and the lawyer attends to present the grounds at the hearing.
  5. For judgments in absentia the start of the period may differ, so the judgment’s details and its service must be examined before relying on any specific date.

Fourth: What the court does after hearing the appeal

OutcomeMeaning
Appeal dismissedThe judgment becomes final and the sentence is enforced
Appeal inadmissible in formFor example, filed late or without a grounds memorandum signed by an admitted lawyer; the court does not examine its merits
Appeal accepted, judgment quashed and remandedThe court sets aside the judgment and sends the case to another circuit of the trial court for a new trial, which may end in acquittal, a lighter sentence, or confirmation of the conviction again
Quashing and deciding the meritsIf the appeal is filed a second time against the same judgment after quashing and remand, the Court of Cassation rules on the merits itself (Art. 39)
Death sentencesThe Public Prosecution must submit the case to the Court of Cassation within a set period (Art. 46), whether or not the convicted person appeals

Fifth: Does the appeal stop enforcement of the judgment?

As a rule, a cassation appeal does not stop enforcement of the judgment, except for death sentences. The law has allowed, in specific cases, an application to the Court of Cassation to stay enforcement, subject to its discretion and conditions; this needs a legal assessment of your case before you rely on it. A convicted person who is in custody therefore generally remains in custody until the appeal is decided, unless the court rules otherwise.

Sixth: Common mistakes that lose the appeal

  • Missing the sixty days while waiting for the full reasons of the judgment, for a settlement that never happened, or on the belief that the period runs from service.
  • Filing a grounds memorandum without the signature of a lawyer admitted before the Court of Cassation, or after the deadline.
  • Relying on factual grounds (“I am innocent” or “the witnesses lie”) instead of legal defects in the judgment and procedures.
  • Overlooking defenses that the defense did not raise before the trial court; some cannot be raised for the first time before the Court of Cassation, so a cassation strategy begins at the trial stage itself.
  • Handing the papers to a lawyer in the last week, leaving no time to read the whole case file and draft serious grounds.
  • Believing promises about the result: no lawyer can guarantee that an appeal will be accepted or a judgment quashed.

Seventh: Why a “file review” matters before appealing

Before you decide to appeal, a cassation lawyer needs to read the court’s judgment, the case papers, the hearing records, and the defenses, to determine whether a real legal defect exists, what the prospects are, what would be asked of the Court of Cassation, and whether another route, such as settlement or a request for clemency, is better. This review is the step that turns the decision to appeal from hope into an assessment. A note on procedure: the new Criminal Procedure Law No. 174 of 2025 has been issued, but its entry into force is postponed to 2027/10/1, so the procedures in this article rest on the provisions currently in force, including Law No. 57 of 1959 on appeals before the Court of Cassation, until they change.

A Question Worth Knowing the Answer To

What is the deadline for a criminal appeal to the Court of Cassation in Egypt?

Answer: Sixty days from the date the judgment is rendered, in the case of a judgment given in the defendant’s presence, under Article 34 of Law No. 57 of 1959 on the cases and procedures for appeals before the Court of Cassation. Within that period the appeal must be declared at the clerk’s office of the court that issued the judgment, and the grounds memorandum must be filed, signed by a lawyer admitted before the Court of Cassation. If the deadline passes, the right to appeal is lost however strong the grounds.

Does the Court of Cassation rehear the case and the witnesses?

Answer: No. The Court of Cassation is not a third instance. It reviews whether the law was correctly applied, whether the procedures were valid, and whether the judgment is adequately reasoned; it does not reassess the evidence or hear witnesses. If it finds a defect it quashes the judgment and sends the case to another circuit for a new trial; if it finds none, it dismisses the appeal.
