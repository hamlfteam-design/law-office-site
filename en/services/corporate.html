Contracting with Companies, Associations and Institutions

We contract with companies, associations and institutions to represent them legally in their various legal disputes as agreed in the engagement contract — with a clear scope of work, written fees and periodic reports on every file.

How Does the Engagement Work?

We start by studying the entity's activity and legal needs, then draft a written engagement contract that defines the scope of legal representation, the disputes it covers, and the follow-up and reporting method. Fees are agreed according to the nature of the entity and the volume of its business, and are set out in the contract before work begins.

Scope of Legal Representation

  • Representation before the courts at all levels and the competent authorities in the agreed legal disputes, including the economic courts.
  • Drafting and reviewing commercial contracts and employment contracts, commercial agency, commercial representation and franchise contracts.
  • Reviewing disciplinary regulations and non-compete and confidentiality clauses for compliance with the law.
  • Legal follow-up of overdue debt collection.
  • Legal advice for the entity, and following labor disputes (contracts, salaries, allowances, termination of service, end-of-service bonus).

Company Formation

We also handle the formation of companies in all legal forms: joint-stock, general partnership, limited partnership, partnership limited by shares, and limited liability — with a legal check-up before formation or partnership.

The scope of representation, the disputes covered and the fee arrangement are all agreed and set out in the contract before work begins.

3 Clauses in an Employment Contract That Get Company Owners into Trouble

  • Contract term: A fixed-term contract renews automatically in certain cases and becomes open-ended — and terminating it then becomes an entirely different matter.
  • Penalties clause: If the disciplinary regulation is not approved and announced to the employee, any penalty you impose may have no legal basis.
  • Non-compete and confidentiality clause: If drafted in a general, open-ended way, the courts narrow it or disregard it — it must be defined by activity, duration and scope.

The Most Common Problems Companies Actually Face

  • Late payment: Customers or entities that delay payment with no clear collection or compensation mechanism in the contract.
  • Contracts not drafted in your favor: Ready-made or literally translated contracts that do not comply with Egyptian law and open the door to disputes over scope, penalties or termination.
  • Wrongful dismissal without grounds: Terminating an employee without proper procedures turns the problem into a costly labor lawsuit.
  • Delays in licensing and registration: Delays in formation procedures, licenses or approvals (including free zones) postpone the start of operations.
  • Compliance gaps: Updates to labor, tax or data-protection law surprise entities that have no in-house legal department.

This is what we take on under the engagement: contract review before signing, labor-compliance audits that prevent wrongful-dismissal suits, follow-up of formation and licensing procedures, legal collection of overdue debts, and representation in disputes as agreed.
