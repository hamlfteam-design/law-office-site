Cybercrime & Online Extortion

If you have been extorted, threatened or had an account hacked, or a cybercrime complaint has been filed against you, we handle the complaint or the defense from the first step through to the final judgment.

Contact Us About This Service

The Key Point

Cybercrime cases are usually decided by digital evidence: messages, links and account data. That evidence can easily be lost if a conversation is deleted or an account is closed. This is why acting quickly and correctly in the first days makes a real difference to the outcome, whether you are the victim or the accused.

Common Cases We Handle

  • Extortion using images, conversations or private information, and threats to publish them.
  • Hacking of accounts and email, and identity impersonation.
  • Online fraud and theft of card and bank account data.
  • Insult, defamation and slander on social media, and violation of the sanctity of private life.
  • Defending the accused in information technology cases and misuse of telecommunications.

First Steps If You Are Being Extorted (In General)

  • Do not pay any amount, and do not negotiate with the extortionist.
  • Do not delete the conversations or close the account the messages came from before they are documented.
  • Keep screenshots showing the account link, the date and the time.
  • The complaint is filed with the Public Prosecution or with the General Department for Combating Information Technology Crimes at the Ministry of Interior, preferably drafted by a lawyer.

Frequently Asked Questions

I am being extorted online. Should I pay the extortionist?

No. Do not pay any amount or negotiate with the extortionist, and do not delete the conversations or close the account the messages came from before they are documented. Keep screenshots showing the account link, the date and the time.

Where is a cybercrime complaint filed?

The complaint is filed with the Public Prosecution or with the General Department for Combating Information Technology Crimes at the Ministry of Interior, preferably drafted by a lawyer. These acts are punishable under Law No. 175 of 2018 on Combating Information Technology Crimes and Article 327 of the Penal Code (threats).

Why does speed matter in cybercrime cases?

Because the case is usually decided by digital evidence: messages, links and account data. That evidence can easily be lost if a conversation is deleted or an account is closed, whether you are the victim or the accused.
