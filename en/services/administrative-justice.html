Administrative Justice & the State Council

Has an unfair administrative decision been issued against you, a license refused, or do you have a dispute with a government body? Time limits are short — start early.

Contact us about this service

The Idea

The administrative judiciary is the route for reviewing decisions of government bodies. Importantly, the time limit for an annulment action is generally sixty days from knowledge of the decision, after which the right may be lost. We review the decision and its grounds promptly, file the appropriate action before the State Council courts, and pursue it up to the Supreme Administrative Court — one of the firm's core areas of practice.

What We Do

  • Actions to annul administrative decisions and to stay their execution.
  • Compensation claims for unlawful administrative decisions and acts.
  • Civil-service matters: promotions, disciplinary penalties and entitlements.
  • Licensing disputes and closure and demolition orders.
  • Administrative contracts and public tender disputes.
  • Appeals before the Supreme Administrative Court.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the deadline to challenge an administrative decision?

The deadline for an annulment action is usually sixty days from learning of the decision, after which the right may be lost — so start early.

Which decisions can be challenged before the State Council?

Administrative decisions and their suspension, compensation for wrongful administrative decisions and acts, public-employee matters, licensing disputes and closure or demolition decisions, and administrative contracts and tenders.

How far do you take the case?

From the State Council courts up to the Supreme Administrative Court — one of the firm's core practices.
