Public Funds Cases

Public funds cases are among the most serious criminal cases and carry heavy penalties. We represent the accused at every stage: the investigation, review of audit and expert reports, applications for release, trial before the criminal courts and, where legally available, appeal to the Court of Cassation.

Contact Us About This Service

Why Early Legal Advice Matters

These cases usually begin with inquiries or lengthy audit and accounting reports, and deciding them depends on a careful reading of the documents and the experts' reports. The investigation stage can be important for preparing the defense, before any referral to court.

Common Cases We Handle

  • Embezzlement and misappropriation of public funds, or facilitating their misappropriation.
  • Bribery and intermediation in it, and influence peddling.
  • Profiteering from public office.
  • Intentional harm to public funds, and harm through gross negligence.
  • Forgery of official documents and their use.

What We Do

  • Attending the investigation before the Public Prosecution and the Public Funds Prosecution from the first session.
  • Studying the reports of the audit authorities and the experts, and requesting documents and seeking further expert review where appropriate.
  • Applications for release, and challenges to pretrial detention and to orders restricting the disposal of assets, where the law allows.
  • Considering whether settlement is available, in the cases where the law permits it.
  • Pleading before the criminal courts at both levels and, where legally available, appealing to the Court of Cassation.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do I need a lawyer in a public funds case?

Ideally, seek legal counsel as soon as you receive a summons and throughout the investigation. These cases often begin with inquiries or lengthy audit and accounting reports, and the investigation stage can be important for preparing the defense.

What are the most common public funds cases?

Embezzlement and misappropriation of public funds or facilitating it; bribery, intermediation in it and influence peddling; profiteering from public office; intentional harm to public funds or harm through gross negligence; and forgery of official documents and their use.

Is settlement possible in public funds cases?

We consider whether settlement is available in the cases where the law permits it. This can only be assessed after studying the file and the reports of the audit authorities and the experts.
