### 4 — إنجليزي — تنفيذ الأحكام (الأربعاء 25 نوفمبر)

**Won a judgment, but nothing has been paid yet?**

In Egypt, a court judgment is not enforced on its own. Enforcement follows set steps in a set order: obtaining the enforceable copy with the executory formula, serving it on the other party, and then attachment through the court bailiffs. A mistake in any step can delay the case or put the enforcement at risk.

Dr. Hassan Abdelmoneim Hassan Law Firm handles:
• Enforcement of civil and commercial judgments and payment orders
• Eviction and delivery judgments
• Enforcement disputes before the enforcement judge, whether you are enforcing a judgment or it is being enforced against you
• Judgments issued abroad, through an enforcement order from the Egyptian courts

Send us a copy of the judgment, and we will tell you whether it can be enforced now and what the next step is.

### 8 — إنجليزي — حقوق الأجنبي الموقوف (الأربعاء 9 ديسمبر)

**Foreign national detained in Egypt? Know your rights.**

If you, a relative or an employee is detained in Egypt, the first hours matter. Under the Egyptian Constitution, the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations, a detained foreigner has the right to:

• Be told immediately why they are being held, and be informed of their rights in writing
• Contact their family and a lawyer straight away
• Be brought before the Public Prosecution within 24 hours
• Have an interpreter if they do not understand Arabic
• Ask for their consulate to be notified without delay, and receive consular visits
• Stay silent until their lawyer arrives, and refuse to sign any paper they do not understand

Our English article sets out these rights step by step, from the police station to the courtroom, together with the most common offences foreigners face in Egypt.

### 10 — إنجليزي — تعاطي المخدرات (الأربعاء 16 ديسمبر)

**Arrested for drug use in Egypt? What the law says**

Many people assume drug use is a minor matter in Egypt. It is not. Under Article 37 of the Anti-Narcotics Law, possessing a narcotic for personal use carries aggravated imprisonment and a fine of EGP 10,000 to 50,000. Where addiction is established, the court may order treatment in a specialised facility instead of serving the sentence.

How to protect yourself:
• Never carry a bag, parcel or item for someone else without knowing what is inside
• Check any car, apartment or item you borrow
• Do not carry controlled medicines without a valid prescription

If you or a family member is accused:
• Do not sign any paper or record
• Do not make any statement without your lawyer present

Our English article explains the penalty, the treatment option and the defence routes in plain language.

**Making a power of attorney from abroad? Know its journey before you start**

A power of attorney from abroad is a simple procedure if done right, and delayed for months if done wrong. Our article walks you through it, from choosing the purpose to the point where it becomes valid before Egyptian authorities.

The journey of your power of attorney:
1. Define the exact purpose: selling real estate, litigation, or settling an estate and inheritance
2. Confirm the correct wording for that purpose before you go to the consulate
3. Have it drawn up and certified at the Egyptian consulate or embassy in your country of residence
4. Have it certified by the Egyptian Ministry of Foreign Affairs inside Egypt, or as the legalization agreements with your country provide
5. Only then is it valid for use before Egyptian authorities

Mistakes that cause delays:
• Assuming one power of attorney will cover all your affairs in Egypt
• General or wrong wording, which gets it rejected by the Real Estate Registry or the court
• Reviewing the text after certification instead of before

**Insulted or defamed online in Egypt? Here is what to do.**

The case begins with a complaint or report that you file, personally or through a special attorney, with the Internet Crimes Police, the Public Prosecution or a police station, within three months of the day you learn of the offense and of who committed it, not from the day of publication. Under the Penal Code, public defamation carries a fine of EGP 5,000 to 15,000 and public insult EGP 2,000 to 10,000; it rises to imprisonment where the act attacks a person's honor or harms the reputation of families.

First steps:
• Take screenshots showing the full text, the account name, the post link, the date and the time
• Save the links and do not delete the conversation
• Do not reply with insults or publicize the incident to shame the offender; that can turn you from victim into accused

Good to know:
• An anonymous account does not prevent you from proceeding
• Service providers retain user data for 180 days, so prompt reporting preserves the evidence

**Murder, killing by mistake or "unintentional killing": what is the difference in Egypt?**

Egyptian law has no offence called "unintentional killing". People mean either battery resulting in death (Article 236), where someone assaults another without intent to kill and the victim dies, or killing by mistake (Article 238), where negligence or recklessness causes a death. Murder requires the intent to take life; its penalty is life or aggravated imprisonment, and it reaches death where there is premeditation, lying in wait or poisoning.

To avoid getting involved:
• Stay away from fights, do not respond to provocation, and do not carry a weapon or tool in any dispute
• Do not drive under the influence of alcohol or drugs or when exhausted, and observe traffic and speed rules
• If an accident occurs, stop, help the injured person and call an ambulance immediately; failing to help when able to is an aggravating factor

If you are accused:
• Do not sign any paper or record
• Do not say a single word except in the presence of your lawyer

### 20 — إنجليزي — الأحد 10 يناير 2027 الساعة 3:00 عصرًا

**Holding a judgment from a court outside Egypt? It is not automatically enforceable here.**

A foreign judgment needs a separate legal procedure, "the order to enforce the foreign judgment", before it has any enforceable force inside Egypt. A suit is filed before the competent court of first instance in Egypt to obtain that order.

Egyptian law requires that:
• The foreign court was competent to hear the dispute in the first place
• The judgment was issued after the opponent was properly summoned to appear
• The judgment does not violate Egyptian public policy

Documents attached to the suit:
• An official certified copy of the judgment
• An accredited translation of it
• Proof that the judgment is final and enforceable in the country where it was issued

Reciprocity is a decisive element: if the issuing country does not enforce Egyptian court judgments, this may affect acceptance of the suit. Any error in the documents prolongs the procedure by months, so precision in papers, translation and authentication matters from the start.

**Civil servant referred for investigation? Know your rights and deadlines**

Under Civil Service Law No. 81 of 2016, no penalty may be imposed on a public employee until he or she has been investigated in writing, heard and allowed to present a defence. The penalties are exhaustively listed in the law.

Your safeguards:
• Knowing the full charge and evidence before being questioned
• Presenting your defence orally or in writing, and being assisted by a lawyer
• Inspecting the investigation papers, and a reasoned penalty decision

The time limits are short and a matter of public policy:
• Grievance: within 60 days of learning of the penalty; 60 days without a reply is an implied rejection
• Disciplinary court challenge: within 60 days of learning of the decision or of the grievance's rejection
• Appeal to the Supreme Administrative Court: within 60 days of the disciplinary court's judgment

If you are referred for investigation, do not sign any paper before you understand it, and do not say a word except in the presence of your lawyer.

**When does an ordinary fight become a felony? Simple battery vs. battery resulting in death**

Simple battery is a misdemeanour punishable by imprisonment of up to one year or a fine (Article 242, Penal Code), where the injury leaves no illness or incapacity for personal work exceeding 20 days. Battery resulting in death is a felony punishable by aggravated imprisonment of 3 to 7 years (Article 236): the assault ends in the victim's death without the offender intending to kill.

Death need not be immediate; the offender is liable if his act caused it. If intent to kill is proved, the offence becomes murder.

How to stay out of trouble:
• Stay away from fights and do not respond to provocation
• Do not use a weapon, stick or tool in any dispute; it increases the penalty
• If someone is hurt, help them, call an ambulance immediately and do not flee the scene
• Resolve disputes through legal channels: a report, a complaint or a lawsuit

If you are accused:
• Do not sign any paper or record
• Do not say a single word except in the presence of your lawyer

**Does your contract have an arbitration clause? What to check before you sign**

Arbitration is the parties' agreement to refer an existing or future dispute to one or more arbitrators of their choosing, who decide it by a binding award carrying the force of res judicata (Art. 55 of Egypt's Arbitration Law No. 27 of 1994). The arbitration agreement must be in writing; otherwise it is null and void (Arts. 10 and 12).

What sets an arbitral award apart:
• It is not subject to appeal or any other ordinary remedy (Art. 52)
• The only route to set it aside is an annulment action, within 90 days of notification of the award and on exhaustive grounds (Arts. 53 and 54)
• Awards are enforceable in more than 170 States under the 1958 New York Convention, to which Egypt acceded in 1959
• It is not enforced automatically; an enforcement order from the courts is required

When drafting the clause, state clearly:
• The institution or rules applied, the seat and the language
• The number of arbitrators and the law governing the contract

An unclear clause can lead to a dispute over jurisdiction before the merits are reached.

**Harm after surgery or treatment: medical error, or death or injury by mistake?**

Under the Penal Code, the result decides the label: if the patient dies it is death by mistake (art. 238); if injured, injury by mistake (art. 244). Law No. 13 of 2025 on Medical Liability and Patient Protection gave "medical error" and "gross medical error" their own provisions, penalties, safeguards and a separate technical review procedure. What decides each case is its facts and the technical expert report, not the mere occurrence of harm.

Key points:
• Not every harm is a medical error; it may be a known "medical complication" despite correct practice
• The Supreme Committee for Medical Liability is the technical expert before investigators and courts; its sub-committees report within 30 days
• Reconciliation under this Law does not affect civil rights

Patients and families:
• Keep the full medical file and ask for a copy
• Do not waive or sign any declaration before legal advice

Doctors and facilities:
• Give no statements without a lawyer, and prepare the documents showing what was done

**Public official in Egypt? Profiteering vs. conferring unlawful gain**

Profiteering and conferring unlawful gain are two forms of a single felony under Article 115 of the Egyptian Penal Code. In profiteering, a public official obtains a gain or benefit for himself from his office; in conferring unlawful gain, he obtains it for another person without right. The penalty is the same: aggravated imprisonment of 3 to 15 years, with removal from office, restitution and a fine equal to the value of the profit or benefit (Article 118).

Key points:
• A mere attempt to obtain the benefit completes the offence, even if no gain is made
• Later repayment does not extinguish criminal liability
• The beneficiary can be liable as an accomplice if an agreement with the official or assistance is proved
• Profiteering is not bribery; they are separate offences

To avoid being implicated:
• Keep your duties separate from your own interests and those of your relatives
• Do not take part in any decision with a private benefit for you or another, and ask in writing for the matter to go to someone else
• If accused, sign nothing and say nothing except in the presence of your lawyer

**Drug trafficking and smuggling in Egypt: what the law says and how to avoid getting involved**

Trafficking in and smuggling drugs are among the most severely punished crimes in Egyptian law. Smuggling, meaning importing or exporting a narcotic without a licence, is punishable by death and a fine of EGP 100,000 to 500,000 (Article 33). Possession with intent to traffic, or trafficking in any form, is punishable by death or life imprisonment and the same fine (Article 34). Where the judge applies leniency, the penalty may be reduced only to the next penalty down (Article 36).

How to protect yourself:
• Do not receive, carry or deliver any shipment, parcel or bag when you do not know its contents, even from someone you trust or for an attractive fee
• Do not allow your name, address, car or storeroom to be used to receive or store items for others
• Do not sign for goods or shipments before verifying their source and contents
• Stay away from any anonymous job or offer that is unusually profitable

If you are accused, do not sign any paper or record, and do not say a word except in the presence of your lawyer.

**Scammed online or by bank transfer in Egypt? How you write the report matters**

Fraud under Egyptian law is a misdemeanor punishable by imprisonment (Penal Code, Art. 336). But it does not arise merely because someone took your money and did not return it: he must have deceived you by fraudulent means, and you handed over the money because of that deceit. A report that only says "he took my money and refuses to return it" often ends up treated as a civil dispute.

What your report should set out, in order:
• Exactly what he claimed, and what made you believe it (a website, an advert, a third person who confirmed his words)
• When and how you paid, with the transaction number and the beneficiary's name
• What happened next: he disappeared, blocked you, or asked for more money
• That you want the account or wallet traced and your right to claim the amount back preserved

What to do straight away:
• Call your bank or service provider and ask for a written reference number
• Take full screenshots before deleting any chat or blocking the account
• Never pay "fees" or a "commission" to someone who promises to recover your money

**Considering khul‘ in Egypt? Know what the wife gives up first.**

Khul‘ is a right of the wife regulated by Article 20 of Law No. 1 of 2000. The wife declares expressly that she detests life with her husband and fears she will not uphold the limits of God, waives all her lawful financial rights and returns the dowry he gave her. If the spouses do not agree on khul‘, the wife may file a claim before the Family Court, even if the husband does not consent.

**Issued a cheque that bounced in Egypt? What the law says**

Under Article 534 of Commercial Law No. 17 of 1999, anyone who intentionally issues a cheque without existing and drawable funds faces imprisonment and a fine not exceeding EGP 50,000, or either penalty. For a repeat offence within five years of a final conviction, the penalty is imprisonment and a fine not exceeding EGP 100,000.

The law allows settlement: the victim or his special attorney may ask the Public Prosecution or the court, at any stage, to record a settlement, which extinguishes the criminal action. If it occurs while the sentence is being served, the Prosecution orders its suspension, even after the judgment is final.

How to protect yourself:
• Do not issue a cheque unless your account will cover it on the payment date
• Do not withdraw the funds after issuing it, and do not instruct the bank to stop payment outside the cases the law permits
• Do not hand over a blank cheque or sign a security cheque without understanding its legal effect

Beneficiary? Keep the original cheque and proof of the bank's refusal, and consult a lawyer without delay.

**Travelling to Egypt and worried about a watch list or travel ban?**

Arrival watch-listing puts your name on lists sent to security at airports and seaports, so you are stopped on arrival and brought before the authority that requested it. A travel ban prevents you from leaving the country. Under Article 62 of the Constitution, a citizen may be barred from leaving only by a reasoned judicial order for a specified period, and may not be barred from returning.

Before you travel:
• Check your status well in advance; do not rely on the electronic inquiry alone
• Find out who issued the decision and why; that decides your route: a grievance to the investigating authority, an administrative court challenge, or an application to the Family Court's Urgent Matters Judge
• Living abroad? Appoint a lawyer by special power of attorney from the Egyptian consulate

Keep in mind:
• Administrative listing: the annulment action is due within 60 days of learning of the decision
• No one may bar a child or spouse from travelling at will; a decision of the competent authority is required
• Do not rely on intermediaries promising removal for a fee; only the competent authority can remove a name

**Lease expired but the tenant is still there? Or no term written at all?**

The term is an essential element of a lease. Where the parties fix a term, the lease ends on its expiry without any need for notice (Civil Code, Art. 598), unless they stipulated that notice be given before expiry. Where no term is fixed, or it cannot be proved, the lease is deemed concluded for the period set for payment of rent, and it ends only upon a notice to vacate served within the time limits prescribed by law.

Key points:
• If the tenant remains in possession with the landlord's knowledge and without objection, the lease is renewed on its original terms but for an indeterminate period (Art. 599)
• Wording such as "for life", "month-to-month" or "as needed" is not a determinate term
• With monthly rent and no fixed term, notice must be served before the last half of the rent period (Art. 563)
• Law No. 164 of 2025 set a seven-year transitional period for residential old-rent leases

**A judgment was issued against you: opposition, appeal or cassation?**

Confusing the ways of challenging a criminal judgment can cost you a deadline, and once it passes the right is lost permanently.

The difference in brief:
• Opposition: against a default judgment issued in your absence without proper notice; the case returns to the same court
• Appeal: against a judgment issued while you were present or properly notified; a higher court re-examines the facts and the law together
• Cassation: not a retrial; the Court reviews the judgment itself: was the law applied correctly, and is it sufficiently reasoned?

Also worth knowing:
• Opposition is not available against a default judgment in a felony (Article 395 of the Code of Criminal Procedure and its counterparts); that judgment falls by force of law once the accused appears or is arrested, and he is retried
• Since Law No. 1 of 2024, the accused may appeal convictions issued by the Criminal Court before the Criminal Appeals Court, within forty days of the judgment

**Did a government body refuse your request? Know the deadline to challenge it**

If a government authority refuses your request by a decision, you can challenge it by an annulment action before the State Council courts within sixty days of its publication or of notice of it to you (Art. 24 of the State Council Law No. 47 of 1972). Even if the authority stays silent and fails to take a decision it was required to take, that is treated as an administrative decision and may be challenged (Art. 10).

What to know:
• A grievance to the issuing authority or its superior bodies interrupts the time limit
• If 60 days pass after your grievance with no reply, that is an implied refusal, not approval, and a new 60-day period starts for filing the action
• Filing the action does not by itself suspend the decision, but the court may order a stay if it is requested in the statement of claim and the consequences of execution may be difficult to remedy (Art. 49)
• You must have a personal interest in the challenge (Art. 12)

Missing the deadline may make the action inadmissible.

**Drug possession in Egypt: why intent changes the whole penalty**

The act is the same, possession or holding, but the intent decides the offence and the penalty. The trial court assesses intent from the circumstances: the quantity seized, how it was prepared, what was found with it, the circumstances of the arrest and witness statements.

The penalties in brief:
• With intent to traffic (Article 34): death or life imprisonment and a fine of EGP 100,000 to 500,000
• For personal use (Article 37): aggravated imprisonment and a fine of EGP 10,000 to 50,000, with the possibility of treatment instead of serving the sentence
• With neither intent (Article 38): aggravated imprisonment and a fine of EGP 50,000 to 200,000

How to protect yourself:
• Do not keep a bag or parcel for anyone before knowing what is inside
• Inspect a borrowed car or an apartment you take over before using it
• Do not carry a controlled medicine without the original prescription

If you are accused, do not sign any paper or record, and do not say a word except in the presence of your lawyer.

**Old rent in Egypt: how much is the rent under Law No. 164 of 2025?**

Since Law No. 164 of 2025, the legal rental value for residential old-rent premises is 20 times or 10 times the rental value in force, depending on the classification of the area, and 5 times for non-residential premises leased to natural persons, with an annual increase of 15%.

Monthly minimum for residential premises:
• Premium area: EGP 1,000
• Medium area: EGP 400
• Economic area: EGP 250

Good to know:
• Until the classification decisions are issued, a provisional amount of EGP 250 a month is paid; the accrued differences are then paid in equal monthly installments
• Areas are classified by survey committees formed by decision of the governor; their decision can be challenged by an annulment action before the State Council courts within sixty days of its publication or of notice of it
• In a Civil Code lease, rent is fixed by agreement, and neither the multipliers nor the survey committees apply
